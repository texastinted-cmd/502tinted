Kentucky limits front side windows to 35% and windows behind the driver to 18%, or 8% on SUVs and vans. Most cars take two to four hours at our shop on Valley Station Road in Louisville.

What tint actually does for a Louisville car

Louisville summers are hot and sticky, and a car parked through a shift at UPS or the Ford plant, or all afternoon at Jefferson Mall, is miserable by the time you get back to it. Ceramic film blocks a large share of the infrared heat coming through the glass, so the cabin heats up slower and the A/C catches up faster.

It also blocks 99% of UV, which is what fades dashboards, cracks leather and burns the arm you rest on the door. It cuts glare from the low sun on the Gene Snyder and I-65 in the morning and evening. It gives you some privacy for what’s on the back seat. And it makes the car look finished.

Picking a shade

Kentucky rates the film on its own, so the factory tint in your glass doesn’t count against you. This is what each shade looks like and where it’s allowed.

How a tint appointment works

  1. 1

    Book a time

    Call or text (502) 631-0419, or send the quote form. Tell us the year, make and model, and whether there’s old film on the glass.

  2. 2

    Drop off

    We look over the glass for chips, old adhesive and dirty seals, then clean every window inside and out.

  3. 3

    Cut and shape

    Film is cut to your glass. The back glass is heat-shrunk so one piece follows the curve.

  4. 4

    Install

    The film goes on the inside of the glass with a slip solution, then it’s squeegeed tight and the edges are finished.

  5. 5

    Final check

    Every edge is checked in daylight, and you get the film, shade and care instructions on your paperwork.

  6. 6

    Pick up and cure

    Keep the windows up for three to five days while the film cures. Some haze or small water pockets in that time is normal.

Why we only install ceramic

Bargain film looks fine for a year or two, then turns purple, bubbles and has to be scraped off. Metal-based films hold up better but can interfere with phone, GPS and key fob signals. Ceramic film uses non-metal particles to block heat, so it stays the same color and doesn’t block signals.

Because Kentucky caps the front doors at 35%, you can’t make the car cooler just by going darker up front. The heat rejection has to come from the film itself, which is why ceramic is the only kind we put on.

Questions we get a lot

How long does car window tint take?

Two to four hours for most cars. Removing old film adds time. Most customers drop off in the morning and pick up the same afternoon.

When can I roll my windows down?

Wait three to five days. In cool, damp weather give it the full five. The film needs time for the slip solution to dry out behind it.

Is it normal to see haze or little bubbles after tinting?

Yes, for the first several days. Those are pockets of water that evaporate as the film cures. If anything is still there after two weeks, bring it back and we’ll look.

Can I get my front doors to match my factory rear glass?

Yes. Front two doors start at $129. Factory privacy glass is dark glass, not film, so we pick a front shade that looks close.

Will tint affect my phone, GPS or key fob?

Not with ceramic film. It has no metal layer, so signals pass through normally.

Do you tint trucks and SUVs?

Yes. They have more glass, so they’re priced by window count. SUVs and vans labeled MPV can go darker behind the driver than cars.
